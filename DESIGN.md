# DESIGN.md

**SoulWithQuran visual source of truth**
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Approved

SoulWithQuran is a premium, tutor-led online Quran education website for English-speaking families and adults. The design system exists to build trust, explain the learning experience clearly, and make a free-trial enquiry feel like a natural next step.

This is a marketing and content website. It is not an LMS, dashboard, SaaS product, WordPress site, mobile app, or student portal. This is not a tutor marketplace, tutor directory, or self-service multi-tutor platform.

The primary teaching model is one-to-one learning. Carefully matched small-group classes may also be offered where appropriate, but the website must never present SoulWithQuran as a large classroom academy or multi-tutor marketplace.

## 1. Design Philosophy

### Trust before persuasion

Visitors should understand who teaches, how learning works, and what they can expect before being asked to enquire. Show evidence, context, and a clear next step. Avoid pressure, false urgency, and exaggerated claims.

### Human over corporate

The tutor, the family learning context, and the care in the teaching relationship are central. Use warm language, authentic photography, and considered details. The site should feel personal and professionally run, never like a faceless education marketplace.

### Clarity over decoration

Every visual element must improve comprehension, orientation, confidence, or action. Remove ornament that competes with the content. Islamic identity should be communicated through purpose, language, photography, and restraint rather than repetitive motifs, mosque silhouettes, crescents, or decorative calligraphy.

### Quality over quantity

Use fewer, better sections, images, claims, and interactions. A calm page with a clear hierarchy is more premium than a page filled with badges, animations, or competing calls to action.

### Editorial spacing

Whitespace is an active part of the brand. Sections should have room to breathe, headings should be allowed to stand apart from body copy, and content should be grouped into clear reading units. The 8px spacing basis is a rhythm, not a reason to make every gap identical.

### Accessibility by default

Accessible contrast, semantic structure, keyboard operation, visible focus, readable type, and reduced-motion support are baseline requirements. Accessibility decisions should be made while composing a section, not added after its visual design is complete.

### Mobile first

The primary experience must work on a small phone with one hand, limited bandwidth, and no hover. Desktop layouts may create more space and editorial balance, but they must not introduce information or actions that mobile users cannot access.

### Calm visual rhythm

The page should move through a consistent sequence: orient, explain, reassure, then invite action. Use a restrained palette, stable alignments, moderate contrast between surfaces, and subtle motion. The interface should feel confident without demanding attention.

## 2. Brand Personality

### Core personality

| --- | --- |
| Premium | Polished hierarchy, high-quality imagery, consistent spacing, and deliberate restraint. |
| Calm | Soft contrast, generous whitespace, quiet surfaces, and no visual urgency. |
| Trustworthy | Clear expectations, transparent wording, dependable interaction patterns, and truthful proof. |
| Warm | Human photography, welcoming copy, warm off-white backgrounds, and gentle sage/gold accents. |
| Modern | Clean responsive composition, precise typography, fast pages, and lightweight interactions. |
| Minimal | Only the content and controls needed for the visitor's decision remain visible. |
| Human | The tutor and learner relationship remain more visible than the technology behind it. |
| Educational | Information is structured for understanding, not just conversion. |
| Personal | The experience should acknowledge different ages, goals, and starting points. |

### Tone and voice

The voice is calm, respectful, clear, and reassuring. Write like a thoughtful tutor speaking to a parent or adult learner: direct enough to be useful, warm enough to feel welcoming, and professional enough to build confidence.

Prefer:

- Plain English and short, purposeful sentences.
- Specific explanations of classes, support, and next steps.
- Reassuring language without guarantees.
- "You" and "your family" when addressing the reader.
- Honest calls to action such as "Book a free trial" or "Ask about classes."

Avoid:

- Aggressive sales language, hype, and false scarcity.
- Unsupported superlatives such as "the best" or "number one."
- Corporate jargon, generic AI copy, or childish language.
- Invented qualifications, numbers, ratings, locations, or testimonials.
- Unapproved Quran quotations, translations, Arabic copy, or decorative religious text.

### Emotional response

The desired response is: "This feels trustworthy, thoughtful, and easy to understand. I can imagine my family learning here, and I know what to do next."

### Brand adjectives

Premium, calm, warm, trustworthy, modern, minimal, human, educational, personal, structured, elegant, spacious, authentic, timeless, friendly, confident, clear, respectful, reassuring, and focused.

## 6. Typography

### Typeface comparison

| Pairing | Strengths | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plus Jakarta Sans + Inter | Warm, contemporary headings with a highly legible and neutral reading face. Matches the approved brand guide and balances premium character with clarity. | **Chosen.** |
| Manrope + Inter | Friendly and modern, but slightly more distinctive and less editorial for long-term content. | Not chosen. |
| DM Sans + Source Sans 3 | Excellent utility and readability, but less premium and less aligned with the approved personality. | Not chosen. |
| Geist + Inter | Precise and native to the current foundation, but more product-technical than human and warm. | Not chosen for the final brand system. |

### Chosen pairing

- **Headings and display:** Plus Jakarta Sans.
- **Body, navigation, labels, and UI:** Inter.
- **Code and technical values:** Geist Mono or the repository's approved monospace fallback when code needs to appear in documentation or tooling.
- **Fallbacks:** `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` for text; `ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, monospace` for code.

The current foundation may use a temporary font setup. When typography implementation begins, the chosen pairing in this document supersedes temporary foundation typography.

### Typography rules

- Use one clear H1 per page and maintain a logical heading order.
- Use sentence case for headings, navigation, buttons, and labels.
- Avoid all-caps except for very short eyebrow labels; use `0.08em` letter spacing and never use it for paragraphs.
- Use `-0.02em` to `-0.03em` tracking for large Plus Jakarta Sans headings when it improves line shape; keep body tracking at `0`.
- Use a maximum reading measure of approximately `68ch` for body copy and `56ch` for hero supporting text.
- Keep paragraphs to two to four lines where possible in marketing sections, but never force a fixed height.
- Avoid manual line breaks unless the wording and editorial composition are intentionally fixed across the relevant breakpoint.
- Use tabular numbers for pricing, schedules, and comparison values when alignment matters.
- Do not use typography as decoration. Every size must communicate hierarchy or improve reading comfort.

## 7. Layout System

### Containers and gutters

| Context | Max content width | Horizontal gutter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small mobile | Content-driven | 20px minimum |
| Large mobile | Content-driven | 24px |
| Tablet | 1024px | 32px |
| Desktop | 1200px | 40px |
| Wide desktop | 1280px | 48px |

Use a centered container with a maximum width of `1280px`. The practical reading and component width will often be narrower. Do not stretch text or cards simply to fill the container.

### Grid

- Mobile: one-column flow with an optional two-column split only for small, closely related controls.
- Tablet: 8-column grid for editorial layouts and 2-column content where it improves comprehension.
- Desktop: 12-column grid with consistent gutters; common compositions are 5/7, 4/8, and 6/6.
- Wide desktop: keep the same 12-column logic and increase breathing room rather than adding content density.
- Align section headings, card edges, media, and calls to action to the same container lines whenever possible.

### Spacing and vertical rhythm

Use an 8px basis:

| Token | Value | Typical use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 0 | 0px | Intentional removal of spacing. |
| 1 | 4px | Icon-to-label micro gap and optical corrections. |
| 2 | 8px | Tight inline spacing. |
| 3 | 12px | Small control and label spacing. |
| 4 | 16px | Default compact gap and input padding basis. |
| 5 | 20px | Mobile card or text grouping. |
| 6 | 24px | Component padding and normal content gap. |
| 8 | 32px | Related group separation. |
| 10 | 40px | Medium content separation. |
| 12 | 48px | Major internal block spacing. |
| 16 | 64px | Compact section spacing. |
| 20 | 80px | Standard mobile section spacing. |
| 24 | 96px | Standard tablet section spacing. |
| 32 | 128px | Standard desktop section spacing. |
| 40 | 160px | Rare, high-breathing editorial separation. |

Section spacing should generally be `80px` on mobile, `96px` on tablet, and `128px` on desktop. Tighten only when two sections are semantically one sequence. Internal component spacing should be smaller than the space between components, and space between major sections should be the largest rhythm in the composition.

### White space philosophy

White space should clarify relationships, not create emptiness for its own sake. Give the most important message the most room. Keep high-intent actions close to the explanation that makes them meaningful.

### Reading and media widths

- Long-form text: approximately `60–68ch`.
- Hero supporting copy: approximately `48–56ch`.
- Form labels and helper text: keep to the width of the associated control.
- Standard content image: use the grid; do not exceed the visual weight of adjacent copy.
- Hero media: may occupy 5–7 of 12 desktop columns; never let it force the message below the fold on small screens.
- Use intrinsic aspect ratios and content-driven height. Avoid fixed heights that crop important faces or break with real copy.

### Layering

Use the following conceptual hierarchy:

- Base content: `0`
- Sticky header: `20`
- Dropdown and mobile navigation: `40`
- Backdrop: `50`
- Dialog or modal: `60`
- Toast or critical temporary feedback: `70`

Do not increase z-index values arbitrarily. Fix the stacking context rather than introducing extremely large values.

## 8. Components

### Buttons

- Primary buttons use Deep Navy fill, white text, a pill radius, and a clear verb: "Book a free trial", "Explore courses", or "Ask a question".
- Secondary buttons use a white or transparent surface with a navy outline and navy text.
- Tertiary actions are text links with a visible underline or a clear color change on hover.
- Minimum target size is `44px` high and ideally at least `44px` wide. Horizontal padding should normally be `20–24px`.
- Use one primary action per visual group. Supporting actions must not look equally dominant.
- States must include default, hover, focus-visible, pressed, disabled, and loading where relevant.
- Do not place an icon in every button. Use an icon only when it improves meaning, such as an external-link or arrow indicating direction.

### Disabled states

Disabled controls must:

- Use `neutral-100` or another approved low-emphasis surface.
- Use `neutral-500` text where contrast remains sufficient.
- Remove hover, pressed, lift, and motion effects.
- Retain readable labels.
- Use `cursor-not-allowed` only as a supporting cue.
- Never communicate the disabled state through opacity alone.

### Cards

Cards are for grouping related information, not for turning every paragraph into a floating box. Use a white surface, `16px` radius, restrained border or small shadow, and `24px` internal padding on desktop or `20px` on mobile.

Interactive cards must have a clear accessible name and a visible focus state. Non-interactive cards should not look like buttons. Avoid nested interactive controls when the entire card is already a link.

### Forms

Forms should feel like a conversation and ask only for information needed to respond or book a trial. Group related fields, explain why sensitive information is requested, and keep the primary action obvious.

- Use visible labels above fields.
- Keep helper text concise and adjacent to the control.
- Show validation in plain language near the field and in a summary when necessary.
- Preserve entered values after an error.
- Never use placeholder text as the only label.
- Clearly distinguish required and optional fields.

### Inputs and textarea

Inputs and textareas use a white surface, `12px` radius, `48px` minimum height for single-line controls, and `16px` horizontal padding. Use a neutral border by default, navy focus ring, and an error border plus explanatory text for invalid states. Textareas should be content-driven with a practical minimum height of approximately `128px` and no forced maximum that prevents a long message from being reviewed.

### Checkbox and radio

Use native semantics and a visible label. The visual control should be at least `20px`, with a `44px` effective touch area created by the label and surrounding spacing. Radio controls are for one choice; checkboxes are for independent choices or consent. Never use color alone to communicate selection.

### Accordion

Use a native button for each question. The button must expose expanded state, retain focus, and make the answer available to keyboard and screen-reader users. Use a restrained height/opacity transition, keep the answer in the document flow, and respect reduced motion. Do not hide important FAQ content behind an accordion solely to reduce page length.

### Badges and tags

Badges are compact labels for category, audience, or state. Use a short phrase, medium weight, `8px` or pill radius, and a pale background with dark text. Use gold and sage as restrained emphasis. Do not create a badge for every feature or claim.

### Alerts

Alerts use a semantic role appropriate to the message, a short title when useful, explanatory text, and an icon only as supporting context. Pair success, warning, error, and information colors with text so meaning is not color-dependent. Do not make alerts look like promotional banners.

### Tables

Use tables only for genuinely comparative data such as schedules, course differences, or inclusions. Keep headers explicit, align numeric values consistently, allow horizontal scrolling on small screens without causing page overflow, and provide an accessible caption or nearby explanation.

### Navigation

The header should establish orientation quickly: logo, a small set of meaningful links, and one clear primary action. Use a white or warm off-white surface. On scroll, a sticky header may gain a subtle bottom border or small shadow; it should not become visually heavy.

Mobile navigation must be keyboard accessible, have a native button with an accessible name, trap focus only when implemented as a true modal dialog, and close predictably. Do not hide essential navigation behind hover.

### Footer

The footer is a calm endpoint, not a second marketing hero. Use Deep Navy as the surface, white for headings and important links, and a readable low-emphasis text color that remains contrast-compliant. Group links into a small number of meaningful categories, include contact or enquiry paths when approved, and keep legal links discoverable.

### Section headings

A section heading typically contains an optional eyebrow, one H2, and a short supporting paragraph. Keep the heading block narrow enough to read as a unit. Eyebrows are optional and should orient the reader; they must not become a repeated decorative label on every section.

### Feature cards

Feature cards should explain one benefit or part of the learning experience. Give each a concise title, a useful description, and an icon only where the icon adds recognition. Avoid generic icon grids that could describe any education business.

### Testimonials

Use real, approved testimonials only. Display the person's first name or approved attribution, relevant context, and the quote without exaggeration. Keep quotation marks and card treatment understated. Do not invent ratings, star rows, locations, or outcomes.

### Pricing cards

Only introduce pricing cards when pricing has been approved and is accurate. State what is included, billing context, and the intended next step. Do not use fake crossed-out prices, countdowns, "most popular" claims without evidence, or visual pressure. If pricing is not ready, use a clear enquiry or trial CTA instead.

### CTA blocks

A CTA block should answer three questions: what happens next, why it is useful, and what action to take. Use a calm navy, sage, or warm off-white composition with adequate contrast. Avoid stacking several competing buttons or using urgency that is not true.

## 9. Radius

Use a small, consistent radius vocabulary:

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| None | `0px` | Full-bleed media, tables, and intentional edge-to-edge structures. |
| Small | `8px` | Badges, compact controls, tags, and small media treatments. |
| Medium | `12px` | Inputs, textareas, alerts, and contained interactive surfaces. |
| Large | `16px` | Cards, feature blocks, testimonial surfaces, and image frames. |
| Pill | `9999px` | Primary/secondary buttons, compact status pills, and approved chips. |

Do not use arbitrary radii between these values without a documented visual reason. Rounded corners should soften the experience, not make every element look playful.

## 10. Shadows

Shadows are a separation tool, not a decoration system. Prefer a border when a surface can be separated clearly without elevation.

| Token | Value | Allowed use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None | `none` | Default for flat sections, footer, dividers, and most text. |
| Small | `0 2px 8px rgba(21, 59, 109, 0.06)` | Default card elevation over the warm canvas. |
| Medium | `0 8px 24px rgba(21, 59, 109, 0.08)` | Important surface groups, open menus, or a selected card. |
| Large | `0 16px 40px rgba(21, 59, 109, 0.10)` | Rare hero media or a prominent CTA group with clear layering. |
| Hover | `0 10px 24px rgba(21, 59, 109, 0.10)` | Interactive card hover only; pair with a small lift. |
| Overlay | `0 20px 56px rgba(21, 59, 109, 0.16)` | Dialogs and focused overlays that must sit above the page. |

Never combine multiple large shadows, colored glows, blur-heavy shadows, or shadows on every element. Hover elevation must not be the only indication that a card is interactive.

## 11. Logo Usage

### Approved usage

- Use the full logo or an approved reversed version in the footer.
- Use the symbol alone only for compact, approved contexts such as a favicon or social avatar.

- Ensure sufficient contrast between the logo and its background.

### Never

- Recreate the logo using text, icons, or CSS shapes.
- Alter its proportions.
- Change individual logo colors.

## 12. Iconography

Use Lucide React as the single default icon family. Its rounded outline language fits the brand's clarity and restraint.

- Default stroke width: `1.75px`.
- Use `2px` for small controls that need stronger legibility; do not mix weights casually.
- Default sizes: `16px` inline, `20px` controls, `24px` standard feature icons, `32px` occasional empty-state or process icons.
- Keep icons optically aligned with their text and use `4–8px` of gap depending on size.
- Use icons to reinforce meaning: navigation, status, direction, form feedback, and recognizable course concepts.
- Provide accessible text for icon-only controls through a visible label or accessible name.
- Prefer one icon style per component family. Do not mix filled, hand-drawn, emoji, and outline icon systems.
- Do not use icons as substitutes for explanation, nor add decorative icons to every heading.
- Do not use mosque, crescent, star, geometric ornament, or decorative Arabic glyphs as a repeating interface motif.

## 13. Photography

Photography is a trust asset. It should make the teaching relationship and learning environment feel real, calm, and attainable.

### Subjects

- The tutor teaching, listening, preparing, or speaking with care.
- Adults and families learning in realistic online-class settings.
- Notebooks, study materials, and quiet home environments when they support a story.
- Muslim families and adult learners reflecting the diversity of the primary target markets, represented respectfully and naturally.

### Lighting and environment

Use natural daylight, soft window light, warm but accurate skin tones, quiet rooms, real desks, and uncluttered home settings. The environment should feel lived-in and prepared, not staged as a corporate office.

### Composition and cropping

Prefer eye-level or lightly editorial compositions with space for copy when the image is paired with text. Preserve faces, hands, and meaningful learning details at mobile crops. Use a consistent aspect ratio within a component family and never stretch a low-resolution asset.

### Expressions and color grading

Expressions should be attentive, relaxed, sincere, and naturally positive. Use restrained warmth and realistic contrast. Avoid orange grading, excessive blur, dramatic spotlights, or filters that make the image feel artificial.

### Avoid

Generic business handshakes, staged headset portraits, unrelated stock classrooms, overly posed children, empty mosque interiors used as generic decoration, visible personal information on screens, misleading before/after narratives, and images that imply qualifications or outcomes not supported by project content.

## 14. Illustration

Illustration is optional and subordinate to real human content. Use it only when it makes an abstract learning process easier to understand or fills a genuine content gap.

Approved illustration direction:

- Minimal, flat, and editorial.
- Simple geometric forms with navy, sage, gold, and neutral colors.
- Rounded outline or restrained solid shapes that align with Lucide icon weight.
- Plenty of negative space and no visual noise.

Do not build a mascot, cartoon tutor, decorative Islamic pattern library, or illustration-heavy identity. Real tutor and learner photography is more valuable for this product's trust goals.

## 15. Motion

Motion should communicate response, continuity, or state. It should never create spectacle or delay access to content.

### Timing and easing

- Micro-interactions: `150–200ms`.
- Buttons, cards, and focus transitions: `200–250ms`.
- Accordion expansion and navigation surfaces: `250–300ms`.
- Use a gentle ease-out for entering and a short ease-in for leaving.
- Do not animate layout continuously or use spring physics unless a future interaction has a clear product reason.

### Interaction rules

- Buttons may change fill, border, text color, or move an icon by a few pixels on hover.
- Interactive cards may lift by no more than `2px` with the approved hover shadow.
- Focus transitions must remain visible and must not depend on hover.
- Accordions may animate height and opacity while keeping content semantically available.
- Mobile navigation may fade or slide a short distance; it must remain quick and cancellable.
- Page transitions should be minimal or omitted. The content should feel fast.
- Loading states should preserve layout dimensions and use a quiet skeleton or progress indicator only when needed.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: remove non-essential movement, use instant state changes or short fades, and never hide content until an animation completes.

## 16. Accessibility

Accessibility is part of the visual system and the definition of done.

### Contrast

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA for normal text, large text, controls, and meaningful graphics.
- Use Charcoal or Deep Navy for primary text. Do not use Warm Gold or Soft Sage base values as body text on white.
- Use pale color fills with dark text for status and badges.
- Do not use color as the sole indicator of state, category, or completion.
- Test text, borders, focus indicators, and disabled states on their actual surfaces.

### Keyboard and focus

- Every interactive element must be reachable and operable by keyboard.
- Use a visible `:focus-visible` treatment with a navy or navy-plus-gold ring and sufficient offset.
- Preserve logical tab order and avoid positive `tabindex` values.
- Menus, dialogs, accordions, and forms must expose their state and labels to assistive technology.

### Touch and controls

- Aim for a minimum effective touch target of `44 × 44px`.
- Provide sufficient spacing between adjacent actions to prevent accidental activation.
- Never rely on hover to reveal essential actions or information.

### Typography and structure

- Support browser zoom and text resizing without clipped content or horizontal scrolling.
- Keep body text at or above `16px` on mobile.
- Use semantic landmarks, one clear H1, logical heading order, native form controls, and descriptive link text.
- Provide meaningful alt text for informative images and empty alt text for decorative images.
- Keep errors adjacent to their fields and identify the correction clearly.

### WCAG considerations

Design and implementation should target WCAG 2.2 AA, including perceivable content, operable controls, understandable forms, and robust semantics. Test with keyboard navigation, zoom, a screen reader where practical, reduced motion, small mobile widths, and long user-generated content.

## 17. Responsive Design

Build mobile-first and adapt composition, not only size.

### Breakpoint intent

Use the project framework's responsive conventions with these design intents:

| Range | Intent |
| --- | --- |
| Small mobile, under 640px | One-column reading flow, full-width actions, compact but comfortable spacing. |
| Large mobile, 640–767px | More breathing room and occasional two-up supporting content where it remains readable. |
| Tablet, 768–1023px | Two-column editorial layouts, 8-column grid logic, and intermediate type scale. |
| Desktop, 1024–1279px | Full navigation, 12-column compositions, and the standard desktop hierarchy. |
| Wide desktop, 1280px and above | Use the full container without adding density; increase negative space when helpful. |

### Breakpoint mapping

- Base: below `640px`
- `sm`: `640px`
- `md`: `768px`
- `lg`: `1024px`
- `xl`: `1280px`
- `2xl`: `1536px`

The primary content container must remain capped at `1280px`, even at `2xl`.

### Responsive rules

- **Mobile:** Stack hero media below or beside the message only when the reading order remains clear. Keep primary CTA full-width or comfortably sized. Use `20–24px` page gutters and approximately `80px` section spacing.
- **Tablet:** Preserve a strong reading line and avoid squeezing desktop card grids into narrow columns. Use `32px` gutters and approximately `96px` section spacing.
- **Desktop:** Use the 12-column grid, a restrained two-column hero, and predictable alignment. Use `40px` gutters and approximately `128px` section spacing.
- **Large desktop:** Keep content at or below `1280px`. Do not enlarge text indefinitely or spread related content across an excessive distance.
- **Navigation:** Desktop links may be visible in the header; mobile navigation must use an explicit accessible control. No essential link may depend on hover.
- **Typography:** Scale from the table in Section 6. Do not let long headings create awkward single-word lines where a sensible width adjustment can solve it.
- **Cards:** One column on small mobile, two columns where readable on large mobile/tablet, and up to three columns on desktop. Maintain equal hierarchy without forcing equal heights.
- **Images:** Preserve focal points, use `next/image` for meaningful raster media, provide dimensions, and define responsive `sizes`. Avoid background images when an informative image can be semantic.
- **Overflow:** There must be no horizontal page overflow. Tables and genuinely wide comparisons may scroll within an announced container.

## 19. Codex Implementation Rules

This document is the visual source of truth for implementation. When a future project source explicitly supersedes a decision here, document the change and use the most recently approved source.

### Before implementation

- Read `AGENTS.md`, the available project documentation, this document, and any relevant approved reference assets.
- Inspect related components, data modules, and existing design tokens before creating new ones.
- State the smallest implementation scope and identify the files that will change.
- Confirm the content is approved or clearly marked as temporary copy.

### During implementation

- Use the approved color tokens, typography pairing, spacing rhythm, radii, shadows, and motion language. Do not invent replacements casually.
- Prefer existing primitives and simple composition. Create a primitive only when a pattern is genuinely reused.
- Keep static marketing content in React Server Components. Use Client Components only for state, browser APIs, effects, event listeners, accordions, navigation, or forms that truly need them.
- Use `next/link` for internal navigation and `next/image` for meaningful raster media with accurate alt text, dimensions, and responsive sizing.
- Use semantic HTML before ARIA. Add ARIA only when native semantics do not express the interaction.
- Use Lucide React for interface icons and keep stroke weight consistent.
- Build mobile-first, then verify small mobile, large mobile, tablet, laptop, and wide desktop behavior.
- Keep client-side JavaScript, dependencies, and animation proportional to the need.
- Treat all form input as untrusted and keep secrets server-side when forms are eventually implemented.
- Implement one route, section, component family, or tightly related interaction at a time.
- Do not expand into future roadmap items while completing the current scoped task.
- Do not modify unrelated components, content, assets, dependencies, lockfiles, or architecture.

### After implementation

- Review the diff and confirm that only intended files changed.
- Run the relevant checks, including `pnpm lint` and `pnpm build` after meaningful code changes.
- Check desktop, tablet, and mobile composition; keyboard flow; focus visibility; contrast; image behavior; long content; and reduced motion where relevant.
- Report validation results honestly. Separate pre-existing issues from issues introduced by the task.
- Record any approved design decision that changes this document before using the new decision elsewhere.

### Source-of-truth hierarchy

When guidance conflicts, use this order:

1. The user's latest explicit instruction.
2. The most recently approved project or brand decision.
3. This `DESIGN.md` document.
4. Existing implementation conventions.
5. General framework defaults.

When an ambiguity does not materially affect product behavior, visual direction, accessibility, security, or public content, make the simplest reasonable choice and keep it consistent with the system above.
